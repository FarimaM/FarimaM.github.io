Research Projects & Professional Experience

Latent Mean–Covariance Modeling for Strategic Foraging

Ongoing

Research Collaborator | Interdisciplinary Schools, Tehran, Iran (Supervisor: Dr. Neda Shahidi, Univ. of Göttingen)

  • Challenge Solved: Addressed the limitations of static population-encoding models to enable joint characterization of time-varying neural dynamics and residual noise correlations in the macaque dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) during self-paced strategic foraging.
  • Methodology: Developed a parallel mean-covariance neural framework combining a Time2Vec-augmented Transformer encoder for conditional mean trajectories with a low-rank latent covariance model governed by temporally smooth Gaussian-process kernels, integrated with permutation-based SHAP and shuffle-null standardized selectivity analyses.
  • Validation: Validated on single-neuron data analysis over 200 ms epoch trials; fine-tuned dynamics of both spatial and strategy-related activities (movement, reward prediction, reward outcomes, action planning), as well as decoding of population covariance beyond mean predictions from task variables.

Multilayer Modeling of Alzheimer's Disease Progression

Spring 2024 - Fall 2026

Research Collaborator | Computational NeuroCognitive Laboratory, Sahand University of Technology (Collaborator: Sobhan Khodadadi, Univ. of Cambridge)

  • Challenge Solved: Facilitated the precise evaluation of functional network reorganization across distinct Alzheimer’s disease stages by transcending the limitations of redundant, single-method connectivity metrics.
  • Methodology: Engineered a 15-method multilayer connectome fusion model coupled with ordinal machine learning and SHAP-driven feature attribution to eliminate single-metric redundancies and isolate primary functional connectivity drivers of Alzheimer’s disease progression.
  • Validation: Evaluated on 201 ADNI participants covering the full clinical spectrum of Alzheimer’s disease dementia.

Connectivity Analysis of Brain Regions for ADHD Diagnosis

Winter 2019 - Fall 2023

Master’s Thesis Research | Computational NeuroCognitive Laboratory, Sahand University of Technology (Supervisors: Dr. Peyvand Ghaderyan & Prof. Mousa Shamsi | Advisor: Dr. Shiva Khoshnoud, Univ. of Tübingen)

  • Challenge Solved: Overcame non-stationary EEG noise and static feature constraints to decode the fronto-parietal/striatal network interdependence that underlies temporal processing deficiencies in ADHD.
  • Methodology: Combined a Sparse Non-Negative Least-Square Coding (SNNLSC) classifier optimized for tiny, noisy datasets with Dynamic Frequency Warping (DFW), a bivariate technique that aligns non-linear sub-band frequency fluctuations across 171 channel pairs.
  • Validation: Assessed via 5- and 10-fold cross-validation on ADHD youngsters and controls during resting and time-reproduction tasks; frontal beta-band connectivity yielded 99.17% classification accuracy.
  • Funding: Supported by the Cognitive Sciences and Technologies Council (CSTC), Tehran, Iran.

EEG-Based Motor Imagery Decoding for BCI Neurorehabilitation

Summer 2017 - Fall 2018

Undergraduate Project | Biomedical Signal Processing Laboratory, Sahand University of Technology (Supervisor: Dr. Sina Shamekhi | Advisor: Dr. Ali Farzamnia, Univ. of Huddersfield)

  • Challenge Solved: Overcame the non-stationary, highly noisy temporal dynamics and linear limitations of traditional static features that hinder robust motor intention decoding for closed-loop post-stroke BCI rehabilitation.
  • Methodology: Engineered a deep learning framework built upon a gated Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) recurrent neural network to process symmetrical inter-hemispheric Cross-Information Potential (CIP) connectivity features, effectively capturing non-linear sensorimotor trajectories.
  • Validation: Evaluated generalization performance using 10-fold cross-validation on the benchmark PhysioNet dataset, establishing stable temporal sequence modeling.
